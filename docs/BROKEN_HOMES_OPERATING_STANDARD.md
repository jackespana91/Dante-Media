# Broken Homes — proposed group-wide impact standard

**Planning document, not an operational or legal policy.** Broken Homes has not been verified as a registered charity/foundation and its programme delivery has not been established.

## 1. Mission
Create practical pathways for people facing family instability, exclusion, poverty, risk of exploitation, unsafe circumstances or lack of opportunity. Focus especially on young people and families and the possibility to rebuild opportunity through supportive relationships, education, employment, sport, music and community partners.

## 2. Contributions from every group brand and joint venture
Each business should agree a documented contribution from one or more categories:

- **Financial:** an agreed share of proceeds/profits or a defined programme grant, never a public number before contract/approval.
- **Professional expertise:** staff time, training, technology, production or design.
- **Opportunities:** vetted pathways to jobs, apprenticeships, training, mentoring or sports participation.
- **Partner access:** introductions to credible local charities, colleges, clubs, employers and community programmes.
- **Awareness:** appropriate fundraising or education campaigns without exploiting stories, minors or beneficiaries.

Build contribution terms into future JV/partnership onboarding only after legal review and agreement. Keep contributions voluntary for external parties unless expressly contracted. Report forecast, committed, paid/received and spent amounts separately.

## 3. Safeguarding first
- Appoint qualified safeguarding leadership and establish appropriate policies before offering beneficiary services.
- Verify each referral partner's qualifications, legitimacy, safeguarding measures and data-protection arrangements.
- Keep beneficiaries' personal information separate from commercial CRMs, marketing lists, gaming platforms and analytics profiling.
- For minors: explicit age-appropriate access, consent/assent, child-protection escalation and restricted record access.
- If an AI conversation assistant is explored, explain it is AI, keep it to safe general support/signposting, add human oversight and professional escalation, and never imply an unstaffed AI can replace qualified crisis or mental-health care.
- Do not advertise gaming, request gambling participation or offer gambling-derived incentives to foundation audiences; prevent cross-targeting.
- Put an immediate-danger notice and verified local emergency routes in place before any live support intake; the staging page currently does not accept intake.

## 4. Outcomes and transparency
Once operational, publish aggregate, privacy-safe outcome metrics (not inflated life-saving claims). Illustrative measures: verified local partners, completed introductions, accepted mentorship referrals, educational placements, apprenticeship/job starts, sports places and follow-through at 3/6/12 months. Separate engagement metrics from actual outcomes.

## 5. Program sequence
1. Governance, jurisdiction, legal status and clear board/trustee accountability.
2. Partner screening in initial target communities, including the UK, Poland, Argentina and Brazil if capacity permits.
3. Pilot one or two clear programmes with vetted providers and defined referral flows.
4. Document risk/quality, learning and outcomes; improve safeguards.
5. Scale responsibly and publish verified impact and financial reporting.
